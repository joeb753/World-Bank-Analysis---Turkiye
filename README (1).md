# Türkiye's Borrowing from the World Bank (IBRD): 2005–2025

This project analyzes Türkiye's borrowing history from the International Bank for
Reconstruction and Development (IBRD), the World Bank Group's main lending arm for
middle-income countries, and compares it against total global IBRD lending over the
same period.

## Data sources

- **Türkiye loan-level data**: World Bank IBRD Statement of Loans (Türkiye-specific extract).
- **Global commitment totals**: World Bank Group Annual Reports (IBRD commitments by fiscal year).

## Key observations

- Global IBRD commitments nearly tripled over the period, from roughly **$14B (2006)
  to $41B (2025)**, growing in two distinct waves: a sharp jump around **2009–2010**
  and a steadier climb from **2020 onward**.
- Türkiye's own borrowing has been far more volatile than the global trend, swinging
  between roughly **$0.4B and $4.9B** a year, but has clearly re-accelerated since
  2022 — its 2024 total ($4.9B) is its highest of the entire period, edging out its
  previous 2010 peak.
- Türkiye's share of global IBRD lending peaked at **~14% in 2008**, but that was a
  one-year spike — it fell back to under 5% the very next year. The current
  **11–13% share in 2024–2025** looks different: it's the tail end of a multi-year
  climb (5.4% in 2022 → 9.2% in 2023 → 13.0% in 2024 → 11.2% in 2025), suggesting a
  sustained trend rather than a single-year outlier like 2008.
